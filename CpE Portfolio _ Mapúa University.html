About me

I grew up taking apart old radios and remote controls, and I never fully stopped. That curiosity led me to Computer Engineering at Mapúa University in Intramuros, where I get to work on both sides of a device: the circuit that senses the world and the code that makes sense of it.

I enjoy embedded systems, IoT, and automation. I am happiest when a project moves from a breadboard sketch to something a real person can use.

Outside class I take part in the university's engineering organizations and enjoy hackathons, pickup basketball, and teaching classmates how to debug.

Personal journey

  1. Year 1: Finding my footing

    Moving into a demanding program meant learning how to study, not just what to study. Programming felt natural, but circuits did not. I started a weekly study group and used simulators to see what the equations were doing.

  2. Year 2: Where hardware got real

    My first microcontroller project burned out two sensors before it worked. Each failure taught me to measure first and assume nothing. That habit now shapes how I debug everything.

  3. Year 3: Building for people

    Team projects showed me that communication matters as much as code. Splitting work, writing clear documentation, and presenting to non-engineers made me a better teammate.

  4. What's next

    I'm aiming for an internship in embedded or network engineering, then board exams and a career building dependable systems.
